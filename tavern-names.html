Fantasy Tavern Name Generator

Every session starts the same way: the party walks into a tavern. Make it one worth remembering — a name with a story already half-attached to it, whether that story is a legend or just a stain nobody will explain.

How to use it

Press the seal for a batch of "The [Adjective] [Noun]"-style tavern names, in the tradition of real and fictional English inns. Draw a handful at once — tavern names are short enough that it's easy to browse several before picking the one that fits your town's size and tone.

Tips for choosing a tavern name

  • A rough, working-class village fits a plainer name; a name involving something ornate (a griffin, a lantern) reads as more upscale or urban.
  • You don't need to explain the name. "The Weary Hound" doesn't require a story about a dog — the mystery is often more useful at the table than the answer.
  • Reusing a tavern name across two different towns in the same setting is a common and realistic touch, not a mistake.

How to pick a tavern name that earns its place

A tavern name does more work than almost any other name in your campaign. It is usually the first proper name your players hear in a new town, and it sets their expectations before you have described a single building: a rough name braces them for a brawl, a grand one makes them check their coin purses, and a strange one guarantees they will ask the innkeeper questions you have not prepared answers for. That is not a problem — that is the name doing its job. Choose the mood first and the name second, using the lists below, or draw a batch from the generator above and then check which section it would have belonged in.

A few working habits from experienced game masters. Match the name to the street: the tavern by the docks should sound dockside even if the party never learns why. Name the innkeeper to match — our character name generator will give you one with an epithet the regulars actually use. Think about what is around and under the place, too: a tavern is often the friendliest door into the local dungeon, and it belongs to a town in a wider realm whose naming style it should share. Finally, write the name on the sign and stop explaining it. Players will invent a better story for The Weeping Widow than the one you had planned, and you can steal theirs.

Frequently asked questions

Why does the format always start with "The"?

It matches the naming convention of most real and fictional English-language inns and taverns, which almost always take a definite article — it's a small detail that makes the name feel authentic.

Can I use these for a homebrew world or published module?

Yes, generated tavern names are free to use in any personal or commercial project without attribution.

Do the names fit any particular fantasy setting?

They're built to fit a generic medieval-fantasy setting, so they should slot into most tabletop RPG campaigns without feeling out of place.

What are some funny tavern names for a D&D game?

The Funny section of the list on this page collects forty joke names built on tabletop in-jokes and pub-sign puns — The Natural One, The Chair Is a Mimic, Lord of the Fries, Beauty & the Beholder. Use them in comic-relief towns, one-shot games, or any campaign where the innkeeper clearly has a sense of humor.

Should my tavern's name match the town it is in?

Yes — the name is often the first thing players learn about a settlement. A dockside name like The Rusty Anchor promises sailors and smugglers, while a noble name like The Gilded Griffin promises prices the party cannot afford. Pick the list section that matches the neighborhood and the name does worldbuilding work before you describe a single street.
