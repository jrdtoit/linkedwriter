Tips for choosing a tiefling name

  • Let the name argue with the character. A paladin called Sorrow or a warlock called Hope tells the table something before the character opens their mouth.
  • A virtue name is a choice, so it should be recent. Ask when your tiefling took it, and what name it replaced — that story is often the character's whole arc in miniature.
  • An infernal name does not have to sound evil. Plenty of tieflings carry a hellish name with pride, or wear it like armour against people who expect the worst of them.

The three tiefling naming traditions

Tieflings have no single naming custom, and that is the point. In most D&D settings a tiefling is named three ways across a life, and each way says something different about where they belong. The first is the infernal name: a name from the bloodline, full of hard consonants and old hellish syllables — Zarviel, Mephistia, Baelthor. It is the name a tiefling is born with, whether they want it or not, and it marks them in every town they enter. Some wear it proudly, as proof of a heritage they refuse to apologise for. Others bury it.

The second is the virtue name, and it is the most tiefling thing about tiefling names. At some point — adolescence, a conversion, a narrow escape — a tiefling chooses a single word to be called by: Hope, Creed, Charity, Sorrow, Vengeance. It is a name as a promise, or a warning, or a goal. A virtue name is never neutral, and that is why it works at the table: the moment you introduce a tiefling called Mercy, every player at the table starts wondering whether she earned it, whether she is living up to it, or whether it is the one thing she has left. The darker virtues — Fury, Sorrow, Vengeance — are just as traditional as the bright ones, and often more honest.

The third is the human-raised name: an ordinary name from the community that raised the tiefling, like Adrian, Celeste, or Silas. Tieflings raised among humans often carry one of these as their everyday name, with an infernal or virtue name kept private — or the other way around, a human name used only at home and a harder name used on the road. For a Baldur's Gate 3 character this is the most flexible option of all: a tiefling Tav with a human name reads as someone who grew up trying to fit in, while the same character answering to an infernal name reads as someone who stopped trying. Either way, the tension between the name and the person wearing it is doing the character work for you.

How to pick a tiefling name that earns its place

Start with the tradition, not the sound. Ask which of the three your character would actually answer to in a crowded tavern — the infernal name they were given, the virtue they chose, or the human name they grew up with — and the list below will narrow itself fast. Then ask what the name costs them: an infernal name invites suspicion in every village, a virtue name invites the table to test whether it is true, and a human name invites the question of what they are hiding. The best tiefling name is the one that keeps generating scenes after character creation is over. If you are naming a whole family or bloodline, pair a first name from one section with a surname from the last section — Ashenborn, Bloodmark, Nightshade — and the house name will do the same work for the family that the first name does for the individual. Frequently asked questions

What are the three tiefling naming traditions?

Tieflings traditionally take one of three kinds of name: an infernal name passed down from their bloodline, a virtue name chosen for themselves — Hope, Creed, Sorrow — or an ordinary human name from the community that raised them. Many tieflings carry more than one across a lifetime, changing names when they change who they are.

What is a virtue name?

A virtue name is a single word — an ideal, a warning, or a goal — that a tiefling chooses as their name, often in adolescence or after a turning point. Hope and Charity are the classic examples, but darker choices like Vengeance, Sorrow or Fury are just as traditional. The name is a statement about who the tiefling intends to be, or what they refuse to become.

Do tieflings have surnames?

Not in the human sense. A tiefling raised among humans usually takes a human family name, while one raised in a tiefling community may use a house or bloodline name — Ashenborn, Bloodmark, Nightshade — or none at all, going by a single name plus a virtue or an epithet.

Can I use these names in Baldur's Gate 3?

Yes. These names are written for any tiefling character, including a Baldur's Gate 3 Tav or Dark Urge run, and for tabletop D&D. The infernal section leans into the Nine Hells flavour the game uses, and the virtue section is built for characters whose name is a choice rather than an inheritance.

Can I use these names in a published adventure or novel?

Yes, generated and listed tiefling names are free to use in any personal or commercial project without attribution.
