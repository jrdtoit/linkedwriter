Dungeon Name Generator

A good dungeon name is half the pitch. This forges names for the crawl, crypt, or ruin your party is about to regret entering — the kind of name that shows up on a tavern rumor board and empties the room.

Press the seal to draw your first names.

How to use it

Press the seal to draw a batch of names, each combining a structural word (crypt, vault, warren, keep) with a condition and, often, a specific theme phrase describing what's actually down there. Draw several at once and keep the ones that fit — the theme phrase is easy to drop if you want something shorter for a passing mention rather than a full dungeon crawl.

Tips for using a dungeon name well

  • Drop the theme phrase for a location your players will only hear about in passing — the shorter name reads more naturally in dialogue.
  • Keep the full name (with theme) for the dungeon they're actually about to enter, since it doubles as a hook.
  • A name that sounds slightly too specific ("of the Ash Serpent") often does more worldbuilding work than a generic one, even if you never explain it directly.

Naming a dungeon by type

The generator above names dungeons the way rumours do — a structure, a condition, and a horror. But the strongest dungeon names start one step earlier, with the type of place, because each type frightens differently. A crypt frightens through a person: somebody specific is buried there, and the name is their last piece of paperwork. A dragon's lair frightens through ownership — the party is not exploring, they are trespassing, and the name should say whose house it is. A flooded mine frightens through industry: shafts, seams and bells, a workplace that drowned mid-shift. A cursed temple frightens through wrongness in a holy register, a bandit hideout through proximity — it is smaller, slyer, and closer to the road than the law admits — and the deep below frightens through absence: no sun, no sky, no names anyone on the surface would recognise.

That is why the list below is sorted by type rather than alphabet. Pick the fear first and the name follows; and when you have the name, the dungeon map generator can draw the place itself, while the fantasy town generator names the settlement whose rumour board points the party at it.

Frequently asked questions

What makes a good dungeon name?

A strong dungeon name usually implies a threat or a story before the party ever sets foot inside — a structure, a condition, and often a specific horror attached to it.

Can I generate a name without the theme phrase?

Some results are generated without a theme phrase for a shorter, punchier name — press the seal again if you want a different mix.

Is this generator only for D&D?

No, the names work for any tabletop system, video game level design, or fiction that needs a dungeon, crypt, or ruin name.

What should I name a dragon's lair?

Name the dragon, not the cave. A lair takes its name from its owner's hoard, breath, or reputation — Vesskar's Hoard, the Melted Mint of Durnessa — because the party is never really entering a place. They are entering somebody's house, and the name should make that feel like the trespass it is.

Can I use these names in a published adventure or novel?

Yes, generated and listed dungeon names are free to use in any personal or commercial project without attribution.
