How to use it

Choose a difficulty to set the size and density of the dungeon — easy maps are small and mostly linear, while deadly maps sprawl across more rooms with multiple loops and side passages. Choose a layout separately: Linear Crawl keeps the path mostly single-file, good for a focused one-shot, while Sprawling Complex branches out and doubles back, better suited to a dungeon the party will explore over multiple sessions.

Each room is numbered so you can jot notes on a separate sheet, and marked rooms show suggested encounter or treasure placement — the generator decides where the tension goes, you decide what's actually waiting there.

Tips for running the map

  • Treat the encounter and treasure markers as placement suggestions, not fixed content — swap in whatever monster or loot fits your campaign and party level.
  • The dashed-border room that appears on Deadly maps is meant to be a secret or hidden area — require a check, a specific action, or a clue to find it rather than showing it to players outright.
  • Print a few at once in a spare five minutes and keep them on hand for whenever a session runs short and you need an unplanned detour.

Frequently asked questions

Are these maps to an exact 5-foot grid?

Each map is drawn on a grid, but room sizes are meant as a layout guide rather than an exact tactical grid — adjust scale to your table's system as needed.

What do the icons on the map mean?

A circled S marks the entrance, a triangle marks the exit or stairs down, a diamond with an exclamation mark marks a suggested encounter, and a small chest icon marks suggested treasure. The legend above explains each symbol.

Does difficulty guarantee a balanced encounter?

No. Difficulty here controls map size, room count, and how many encounter and treasure markers appear — the game master still needs to choose what actually occupies each marked room based on their system and party level.
